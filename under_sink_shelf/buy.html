What the comparison found

  • Castorama versus Amazon overall: the timber is only at Castorama, so a Castorama trip is needed either way. Buying everything else on Amazon too costs more (about €122 against €111), mostly because of clamps, brush and sandpaper. The cheapest mix is Castorama for everything except the brackets.
  • Screws: small packs win. You need about 24 of the 4 × 40 mm (12 kitchen, 8 closet riser, 4 jar riser). Castorama's own Diall A2 screws also come in packs of 20 at €5.89; two packs (€11.78) leave spares and cost €8 less than the 200-pack at €19.90. Les Ulis had one pack of 20 in stock on 28 Oct, so order the second for pickup or delivery. If you order on Amazon anyway, 200 A2 Torx 4 × 40 cost €14.90; a 50-pack costs €11.92, so there the big pack is the better deal.
  • Timber: Castorama only. I found no like-for-like 18 mm × 40 × 200 cm pine board or 27 × 27 × 2.4 m pine batten on Amazon. The results were craft strips and cutting boards.
  • Brackets: Amazon is cheaper and better. VFANDV's pack of 20 stainless 40 × 40 × 16 mm brackets with screws is €7.79, dispatched by Amazon. Castorama's pack of 25 is galvanised, €13.90, and has no screws. You need 9 for the kitchen and 4 for the riser. The listing photo shows the screws as 15 mm long, short enough not to come through an 18 mm shelf, and 4 holes per bracket. The title says 80 screws but the photo says 60; 13 brackets need 52.
  • Tools and consumables: Castorama is cheaper. Clamps, sandpaper and a brush all cost less there. The crosscut saw is the exception: Stanley's 500 mm JetCut fine is €16.92 on Amazon against €17.90 for the 450 mm one at Castorama. It is picked at Castorama because €1 does not cover Amazon's small-order delivery.
  • Linseed oil: Amazon does not list the Phébus 1 L. The closest 1 L is a baked linseed oil at €9.30 with 8 reviews.

Notes on the picks

  • The brackets go on the three post-to-shelf joints of each shelf. A 16 mm wide bracket is enough, because the posts are 27 mm wide.
  • The batten page lists a bevelled edge, so the posts will have slightly eased edges.
  • For the damp cabinet the glue should be water-resistant (D3). The Pattex Power pick is rated "water resistant", with no D-class on the listing. Sader Pro D3 (100 g, €9.89) is the one that states D3.
  • A pilot drill bit (2.5 mm) and a countersink are not on the list. Add them if you do not have them.
  • The saw is a fine-tooth crosscut saw rather than the coping saw on earlier versions of this list: the top-shelf notch it was for is gone, and every cut left is straight. The fine teeth leave a cleaner edge on the pine.
  • Jar riser for the kitchen top closet: a 600 × 400 mm top on four 20 mm dowel legs, 162 mm long, 18 cm tall overall. The legs come from your two 1 m dowels, so it costs nothing extra. The top fits on the same board only if the two kitchen shelves are cut nested (turned 180° to each other, see the cut plan on the Design tab), which brings them from 860 mm of board down to 618 mm. Fix each leg with one 4 × 40 screw down through the top into a 2.5 mm pilot hole in the centre of the dowel end, plus glue. After all three pieces, about 175 mm of batten and the dowel offcuts are left.
  • Closet riser: a 750 × 400 mm top (the closet is about 76 cm wide with obstacles) on four 27 × 27 mm legs, 182 mm long, 200 mm tall overall. Fix each leg with two 4 × 40 screws down through the top. Brackets and glue are optional on the riser: its legs are short and only carry weight straight down. The cut plan is in closet_riser.py.
