Misc / under-sink shelf

Assembly, step by step

Design Cost & buy list Découpe Assembly Folding table

From cut parts to a finished shelf: two shelves, three 300 mm base posts, three 182 mm middle posts, 9 stainless brackets, 6 screws 4 × 40 and 36 short bracket screws, plus the Pattex glue. All sizes in mm. Assemble first and oil afterwards: glue does not stick to oiled wood.

red outline: the parts added in that step

0. Before anything is glued

  • Dry-fit in the cabinet. Stand the parts in place without glue. The unit is as wide as the door, so it goes in turned.
  • Measure the cabinet floor to the bottom of the basin. The design assumes 600 mm, which leaves 82 mm above the top shelf. If it is under about 540 mm, stop and shorten the middle posts first.
  • Look at the back for the trap and the dishwasher hose. Neither may sit where the top shelf or a post goes.
  • Lay the two shelves one on the other and check they match, then write TOP on the face that will be up on each.

1. Mark the post centres (both shelves)

On the TOP face of each shelf, mark the three centres. Both shelves get the same marks, because they have the same outline and go in the same way round.

PostMeasureand
Back-right22.5 from the back edge22.5 from the long side
Front-right29.5 from the front edge22.5 from the long side
Back-left22.5 from the back edge39.5 from the 63 mm edge

Each post is a 25 mm square whose edges sit 10 mm in from the back edge and the long side (17 mm from the front edge and 27 mm from the 63 mm edge). The centre is 12.5 mm further in. Mark it with a square, not by eye.

2. Drill the shelves

  1. At each centre, drill 4 mm right through the shelf, with a scrap board underneath so the wood does not splinter where the bit comes out. Six holes in all.
  2. Countersink each hole on the TOP face so the screw head sits flush.
  3. Turn each shelf over and draw a 25 mm square centred on each hole (12.5 mm each way). The posts are glued onto these squares.

The 4 mm hole is wider than the screw, so the screw grips only the post and pulls the shelf down tight on it. The 2.5 mm pilot into the post is drilled later, through this hole, once the post is in place.

Bracket screws: 16 mm long at most on the shelf side. Wrap tape round the 2 mm bit about 12 mm from the tip as a depth stop.

3. Middle shelf onto the three 300 mm base posts

  1. Put the middle shelf upside down on the table (TOP face down).
  2. Glue the end of a base post. End grain soaks up glue: spread a thin coat, wait about 5 minutes, add a second coat.
  3. Stand the post on its square and press it down.
  4. Fit a bracket in the corner between the post and the shelf, on the post face that points into the shelf so its flat leg points into the shelf, never toward an edge: back-right on the post's front face, back-left on its right-hand face (the leg runs along the back wall, not toward the diagonal), front-right on its back face. Use the same face at every level. (Some are hidden behind their post in the drawings.)
  5. Pilot 2 mm (tape stop at about 12 mm) and drive the short screws: 2 into the shelf, 2 into the post.
  6. Do the other two posts the same way.
  7. Turn it the right way up, standing on its posts on the floor.
  8. Through each 4 mm hole, drill a 2.5 mm pilot about 25 mm into the post, then drive a 4 × 40 screw.
  9. Wipe any glue that squeezes out with a damp cloth: oil will not take where glue has dried.

4. The three 182 mm middle posts

  1. On the top of the middle shelf, glue each middle post straight above a base post, covering its screw head (the square drawn in step 2 is now under it: use the screw head as the centre).
  2. Fix each with a bracket on the same face as the bracket below, with short screws as before.

These posts get only glue and a bracket at their bottom ends: a screw from below would hit the one coming down from above.

5. The top shelf

  1. Put the top shelf upside down on the table.
  2. Glue the tops of the middle posts (two thin coats).
  3. Turn the lower part upside down and set it on the top shelf, each post on its square.
  4. Fit the last 3 brackets. They are easy to reach this way up.
  5. Turn the whole shelf the right way up.
  6. Drill the 2.5 mm pilots through the 4 mm holes into the post tops and drive the last three 4 × 40 screws.

Heights from the cabinet floor: middle shelf underside 300, top 318; top shelf underside 500, top 518.

6. Check, cure, then oil

  1. Stand it on the floor and press on each corner. If it rocks, one post is a little long: sand its foot on paper taped to a flat surface, holding the shelf upright.
  2. Let the glue cure for the time on the Pattex bottle (usually 24 hours).
  3. Oil with the linseed oil: thin coats on every face, wipe off all the excess after 15–20 minutes, extra coats on the end grain (the diagonal and the post feet), about 24 hours between coats. Wipe oil off the brackets.
  4. Oily rags can catch fire on their own. Hang them flat outside to dry hard, or keep them in a sealed jar of water.
  5. Let it cure at least two weeks somewhere dry before it goes under the sink.
